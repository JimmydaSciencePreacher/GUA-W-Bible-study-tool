# Scripture Cross-Reference

## Purpose

This file provides a disciplined way to follow connections within Scripture. A cross-reference is a path for investigation, not automatic proof that two passages mean the same thing or that a proposed interpretation is correct.

## Relationship to Creation investigation

Scripture cross-references are one evidence stream in the unified investigation. They remain internal biblical evidence and must be read in context. They do not replace examination of Creation, and scientific observations do not replace exegesis. If a cross-reference raises a question about Creation, record and investigate it separately using appropriate methods.

## Governing practice

1. Begin with the passage being studied.
2. Read the surrounding verses and the larger section.
3. Identify the subject, speaker, audience, action, command, promise, warning, and stated result.
4. Follow references to related passages, reading each in its own context.
5. Record both agreement and tension. Do not erase a passage because another passage is difficult to reconcile.
6. Distinguish the biblical wording from the study's inference or interpretation.
7. Keep unresolved connections open rather than forcing a conclusion.

## Recording a cross-reference

For every connection, record:

- **Primary passage:** the text currently being studied.
- **Related passage:** the passage being compared.
- **Shared subject or wording:** what makes the connection worth examining.
- **Context of the related passage:** speaker, audience, occasion, and surrounding argument.
- **Relationship observed:** agreement, development, contrast, clarification, or unresolved tension.
- **Conclusion status:** textual statement, observation, inference, interpretation, or unresolved question.

## Safeguards

- A shared word does not by itself establish a shared meaning.
- A related subject does not make two passages interchangeable.
- Do not isolate a verse from its context.
- Do not use a cross-reference to silence a warning, weaken a promise, or remove a difficult statement.
- Let Scripture's own wording set the limits of the claim being made.
