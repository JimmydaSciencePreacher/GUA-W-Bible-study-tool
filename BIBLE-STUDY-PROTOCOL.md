# Bible Study Protocol

## Purpose

This protocol establishes a repeatable way to study Scripture using GUA-W. It protects the distinction between the biblical text, the map used to organize observations, and the Witness who reports what is understood.

## 1. Establish the passage

- Identify the primary passage and use the King James Version (KJV) as the foundational study text.
- Read the complete immediate section, not only a selected verse.
- Identify the book's subject and argument.
- Note speaker, audience, occasion, commands, promises, warnings, and stated outcomes.

## 2. Observe before interpreting

Record the wording and structure of the passage before proposing what it means.

Notice repeated terms, contrasts, sequence, stated causes and results, and boundaries. Do not turn an observation into a conclusion without explaining the reasoning.

## 3. Follow Scripture's cross-references

Use the [Scripture Cross-Reference](SCRIPTURE-CROSS-REFERENCE.md) guide. Read each related passage in its own context. Record agreement, development, contrast, clarification, and unresolved tension. A cross-reference is an invitation to examine, not a shortcut around context.

## 4. Use the Four Questions

Ask all four questions together and return to them as the study develops:

1. **What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?**
2. **What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?**
3. **What am I learning about what I am witnessing?**
4. **What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?**

Do not treat them as sequential stages. Let each question inform the others.

## 5. Apply GUA-W without altering it

Preserve the governing order:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

Use established GUA-W terms only where they help describe what the passage presents. Do not force every architectural layer into every passage. If a term or relationship is not sufficiently defined, preserve it as an open question rather than inventing an answer.

## 6. Classify statements

Keep these categories distinct:

- **Text:** what Scripture explicitly states.
- **Observation:** what is directly noticed in the text.
- **Inference:** what is reasoned from observations.
- **Interpretation:** an explanation of the text's meaning.
- **Unresolved:** what the study has not settled.

Make clear which category supports each important conclusion.

## 7. Apply the Triple Constraint Law

Review claims for biblical compliance, scientific coherence where a scientific assertion is actually made, and GUA-W consistency.

This repository studies the Bible; it does not conduct science investigations. A scientific question that needs investigation is outside this tool's scope. Do not claim that science proves Scripture.

## 8. Preserve unresolved questions

Do not force closure to make a study appear complete. Record competing readings, missing information, contextual tensions, and questions requiring further Scripture study. Use [Unresolved Questions](UNRESOLVED-QUESTIONS.md).

## 9. Correct transparently

When a prior statement is corrected:

- Preserve what was previously recorded where useful for continuity.
- State the corrected wording.
- Explain what prompted the correction.
- Identify whether the change concerns text, observation, inference, interpretation, or GUA-W terminology.
- Do not silently rewrite an unresolved question as a settled conclusion.

## 10. Close the study record

Summarize what the text establishes, what remains interpretive, and what is unresolved. Identify the next passage or question to examine. Another Witness should be able to follow the reasoning without being required to accept an unsupported conclusion.
