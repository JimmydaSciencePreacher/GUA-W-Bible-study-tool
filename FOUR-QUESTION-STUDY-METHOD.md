# Four-Question Study Method

## Purpose

The Four-Question Study Method is the recurring inquiry method for using GUA-W in Bible study. The questions help AI and the human Witness examine the text, understand the map, and examine the act and limits of witnessing without confusing the Witness with the Author.

## The four questions

### 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

This question identifies what AI must clarify before applying GUA-W: terms, definitions, relationships, boundaries, governing principles, and what is or is not established. AI must ask rather than invent a missing definition or silently redesign the architecture.

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

This question asks what the established GUA-W map helps make recognizable in the passage. It may clarify distinctions, order, relationships, constraints, or the position of a Witness. The map is an aid to understanding, not a replacement for Scripture and not an authority above the Source.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

This question turns attention to the subject being witnessed. What does the biblical text state in context? What is observed or measured in Creation? What do relevant scientific methods and sources establish, and what remains model-dependent or uncertain? Keep textual evidence, Creation observations, scientific models, inference, and interpretation distinct.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

This question examines the Witness's reception, comprehension, limits, interpretation, and reporting. A Witness may faithfully report what it detects while providing an incomplete account of what is present. Limited comprehension does not, by itself, establish misalignment. Do not use this question to judge another person's heart.

## The questions work together

The four questions are not four sequential stages, a checklist that ends after one pass, or four independent tests. They are recursive and mutually informative.

A passage may reveal a question AI had not known to ask. GUA-W may clarify a relationship that changes how the passage is organized. The passage may expose a limitation in the Witness's prior understanding. Examining the Witness may reveal where a report needs qualification or where an unresolved question must remain open.

Return to all four questions as understanding develops. Preserve the earlier record so that changes in understanding are visible rather than silently substituted.

## Practical use

1. Read the KJV passage in its immediate context and record what it explicitly says.
2. Define the Creation question(s) raised by the passage or investigation.
3. Investigate relevant Creation evidence using appropriate disciplines, methods, and sources.
4. Follow Scripture cross-references in their own contexts; examine relevant linguistic, historical, and textual evidence.
5. Compare evidence streams only after examining each on its own terms.
6. Work through all four questions recursively and identify GUA-W relationships without forcing the architecture onto the evidence.
7. Apply the Triple Constraint Law to claims proposed for GUA-W placement.
8. Classify text, observation, measurement, model, inference, interpretation, and unresolved matters; record what changed and why.

## Guardrails

- AI is an instrument for retrieval, analysis, comparison, and mapping; it is not the Author.
- AI must not invent or independently alter GUA-W.
- The human Witness is not made the source of the truth being studied.
- A Witness's report can be incomplete without every detected statement being false.
- Do not treat disagreement, a question, or limited comprehension as proof of spiritual misalignment.
- Do not force a conclusion where the text and its context have not settled the matter.
