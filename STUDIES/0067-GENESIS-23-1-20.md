# Study 0067 — Genesis 23:1–20 (KJV)
## Sarah's death, Abraham's mourning, and the purchase of Machpelah

- **Primary passage:** Genesis 23:1–20 (KJV)
- **Previous study:** [Study 0066 — Genesis 22:20–24](0066-GENESIS-22-20-24.md)
- **Next passage:** Genesis 24:1–67 (KJV), Abraham's servant and the search for Isaac's wife
- **Central question:** What does this passage reveal about mortality, mourning, promise, sojourning, public agreement, and the actual acquisition of a burial place?

## 1. Immediate context

Genesis 22 recorded the test of Abraham, God's intervention, the ram offered in Isaac's place, and the reaffirmation of God's promise. Genesis 22:20–24 then reported Nahor's family and identified Bethuel as Rebekah's father. Genesis 23 turns to Sarah's death and Abraham's purchase of a burial place.

The passage records:
- Sarah's age and death at 127 years.
- Abraham's mourning for Sarah.
- Abraham's request to the children of Heth for a burial possession.
- The people's recognition of Abraham as a mighty prince among them and their offer of burial places.
- Abraham's request for the cave of Machpelah belonging to Ephron.
- Ephron's public offer of the field and cave, followed by Abraham's insistence on paying.
- The stated price of four hundred shekels of silver.
- The weighing of the silver and the public confirmation of the transaction.
- Abraham's burial of Sarah in the cave of the field of Machpelah.

The central distinction is between a promise concerning land and the specific property Abraham actually acquires in this passage. Genesis records God's broader promise elsewhere; Genesis 23 records a concrete purchase for a burial place. The purchase must not be inflated into possession of the entire promised land.

## 3. Verse 1 — Sarah's lifespan

Genesis 23:1 states: “And Sarah was an hundred and seven and twenty years old: these were the years of the life of Sarah.”

### Direct observations

- Sarah's age at death is stated as 127 years.
- The verse emphasizes “the years of the life of Sarah.”
- The narrative gives a precise lifespan before reporting her death in verse 2.

Sarah is the only woman in Scripture whose age at death is explicitly stated in this way. That observation concerns the biblical text; it does not establish why the narrator gives this detail.

The passage does not give Sarah's birth date or a modern calendar chronology. The stated age should be preserved without manufacturing a date.

### Life, time, and the record

The verse registers a completed lifespan. The narrator records the measure supplied by the text. It is not the narrator who gives Sarah her life or determines its duration.

For GUA-W purposes, this is a distinction between the event and its registration: Sarah's life has reached its recorded endpoint; the text witnesses that endpoint by giving her age.

## 4. Verse 2 — Sarah dies; Abraham mourns

Sarah dies in Kirjath-arba, “the same is Hebron in the land of Canaan.” Abraham comes to mourn for Sarah and to weep for her.

### Direct observations

- Sarah dies at Kirjath-arba.
- The verse identifies Kirjath-arba with Hebron.
- The location is in the land of Canaan.
- Abraham mourns and weeps for Sarah.

The passage does not present Abraham as emotionally detached. His mourning and tears are explicitly recorded. Faith and grief are not treated as mutually exclusive in this account.

The text does not specify the exact length of the mourning, the cause of Sarah's death, or the precise circumstances immediately preceding it.

### Do not turn grief into a failure

Nothing in these verses calls Abraham's mourning disobedience or a lack of faith. The text simply records the death of Sarah and Abraham's grief. We should not impose a judgment the passage does not make.

## 5. Verses 3–4 — Stranger and sojourner; a request for a burial possession

Abraham rises from before his dead and speaks to the children of Heth. He says: “I am a stranger and a sojourner with you: give me a possession of a buryingplace with you, that I may bury my dead out of my sight.”

### Direct observations

- Abraham rises from before Sarah's body.
- He addresses the children of Heth.
- He describes himself as a stranger and sojourner among them.
- He asks for a burial possession.
- His immediate purpose is to bury Sarah.

The words “stranger” and “sojourner” describe Abraham's stated position among the people in this transaction. They should not be replaced with an assumption that Abraham owns no property anywhere or has no standing among them. The passage itself will show that he can negotiate a public purchase.

### Promise and present possession

Earlier Genesis passages record God's promises concerning the land (Genesis 12:1–7; 13:14–17; 15:18–21; 17:8). Here, Abraham still describes himself as a stranger and sojourner and asks to acquire a particular burial place.

This gives us a precise distinction:
- God's promise is recorded in the earlier covenant narrative.
- Abraham's stated status in this negotiation is that of a stranger and sojourner.
- The property acquired here is a particular field and cave, not the whole land of Canaan.

The text does not say that the promise has failed because Abraham purchases a burial place. Nor does it say that this purchase completes the entire promise.

## 6. Verses 5–6 — The children of Heth answer

The children of Heth answer Abraham, calling him “a mighty prince” among them. They offer him the choice of their sepulchres and say none will withhold a burial place from him.

### Direct observations

- The people answer Abraham.
- They describe him as a mighty prince among them.
- They offer him access to their burial places.
- They say none will withhold a sepulchre from him.

The exchange is respectful. The text does not record a hostile dispute at this point.

The people's offer is broad and generous in wording. Abraham nevertheless proceeds to ask for a specific property and later insists on paying for it.

### Recognition is not ownership

The people recognize Abraham's standing among them, but their recognition is not itself the completed transfer of the field and cave. The transaction continues through a specific request, a stated price, payment, and public confirmation.

## 7. Verses 7–9 — Abraham bows and names the property

Abraham stands up, bows to the people of the land, and asks them to speak to Ephron the son of Zohar on his behalf. Abraham requests the cave of Machpelah, which belongs to Ephron and is at the end of his field. Abraham asks that Ephron sell it to him for its full price as a burial possession.

### Direct observations

- Abraham bows before the people of the land.
- He asks them to approach Ephron on his behalf.
- Ephron is identified as the son of Zohar.
- The cave is called Machpelah.
- The cave belongs to Ephron.
- It is located at the end of Ephron's field.
- Abraham requests a sale at its full price.
- The stated purpose is a burial possession.

The request is specific. Abraham does not merely ask for permission to use an unspecified grave. He identifies the owner, the cave, its relationship to the field, and the form of transaction he seeks.

The passage does not provide measurements of the field or a survey description sufficient to reconstruct its modern boundaries. The boundaries and property components named in verses 17–18 are the limits of the biblical record here.

## 8. Verses 10–11 — Ephron's public answer

Ephron is sitting among the children of Heth. He answers Abraham in the hearing of the people and offers the field and the cave within it. He says he gives them to Abraham and invites him to bury his dead.

### Direct observations

- Ephron is present among the children of Heth.
- He answers publicly.
- The response is heard by those entering the gate of the city.
- Ephron offers the field and the cave in it.
- His words describe the offer as a gift.

The public setting matters because the next verses continue the negotiation and the final transfer is confirmed before the people. The passage does not provide a complete legal code or explain every custom governing property transfers in that society.

### Offer and completed transaction are not identical

Ephron's initial words offer the field and cave. Abraham then responds by insisting on payment. The narrative proceeds to the price, weighing of silver, and confirmation of the property as Abraham's possession.

We should not stop at verse 11 and claim the final form of the transaction has already been established. The complete sequence matters.

## 9. Verses 12–13 — Abraham insists on paying

Abraham bows before the people and speaks to Ephron in their hearing. He asks Ephron to accept the money for the field, so that he may bury Sarah there.

### Direct observations

- Abraham bows again.
- He speaks in the hearing of the people.
- He asks Ephron to accept payment.
- He explicitly connects payment with the acquisition of the burial property.

Abraham does not simply accept the offer as a gift. He insists on paying. The text records his action but does not state all of his private reasons.

It is reasonable to observe that the narrative presents a deliberate purchase rather than a burial based solely on informal permission. It would go beyond the text to claim certainty about every political, legal, or strategic motive behind Abraham's insistence.

## 10. Verses 14–16 — Four hundred shekels of silver

Ephron answers that the land is worth four hundred shekels of silver and asks what that amount is between them, then tells Abraham to bury his dead. Abraham agrees and weighs out the silver named by Ephron, in the hearing of the children of Heth: four hundred shekels of silver, current money with the merchant.

### Direct observations

- Ephron names a price of four hundred shekels of silver.
- Abraham agrees to the stated amount.
- Abraham weighs out the silver.
- The payment is made in the hearing of the children of Heth.
- The KJV describes the silver as “current money with the merchant.”

The text gives the ancient unit and amount but does not provide a modern weight conversion in the passage. A conversion would require decisions about the historical standard being used and should not be stated as an exact modern equivalent without supporting evidence.

The wording “current money with the merchant” indicates an accepted standard of weighed silver in the transaction. The verse does not describe modern coinage or paper currency.

### A completed exchange requires more than a spoken offer

The narrative records a specific price and payment. The transaction is not represented merely by Abraham's desire to own the property or Ephron's offer. The silver is weighed and the transaction proceeds to the formal confirmation of the property.

## 11. Verses 17–18 — The property is made sure

The passage identifies the field of Ephron in Machpelah, which was before Mamre, including the field, the cave, and all the trees within the field and in all the borders round about. It states that these were made sure unto Abraham for a possession in the presence of the children of Heth and before all who entered the gate of the city.

### Direct observations

The property description includes:
- The field of Ephron in Machpelah.
- The cave within the field.
- The trees within the field.
- The boundaries round about.
- Public confirmation in the presence of the children of Heth.
- The presence of those entering the gate of the city.

The text uses the language of a possession being made sure to Abraham. This is the narrative's explicit statement that the transaction has reached its recognized result.

### The difference between promise and acquired property

This is the central distinction of the chapter.

The earlier promise concerning the land is not identical to this purchase. Abraham acquires a defined burial property through a transaction. The text does not say he thereby owns all Canaan.

The purchase is real and specific. The larger promise remains larger than the property described here.

### Witnesses and public registration

The children of Heth and those at the city gate are present for the transaction. The passage therefore records a public acknowledgment of the purchase.

For GUA-W, we can observe a distinction between:
- The property and its owner before the transaction.
- The request and negotiation.
- The stated price and payment.
- The public confirmation of the resulting possession.

The witnesses do not create the field or author God's promise. They are present for the public recognition of the transaction described in the text.

## 12. Verses 19–20 — Sarah is buried; the purchase is confirmed

Abraham buries Sarah in the cave of the field of Machpelah before Mamre, identified as Hebron, in the land of Canaan. Verse 20 concludes that the field and cave were made sure unto Abraham for a possession of a buryingplace by the children of Heth.

### Direct observations

- Abraham buries Sarah.
- The burial place is the cave in the field of Machpelah.
- The location is before Mamre, identified as Hebron.
- The land is Canaan.
- The final verse repeats that the field and cave were made sure to Abraham.
- The purpose is identified as a burial possession.

The conclusion repeats the legal and property result after the burial itself. The passage thus closes both the human event—Sarah's burial—and the transaction—the confirmed possession.

### The burial place in the continuing narrative

The cave of Machpelah becomes a significant family burial place in later Genesis passages. Genesis 25:9 records Abraham's burial there; Genesis 49:29–32 and 50:13 refer to the family burial place and its purchase.

These later references confirm the continuing importance of the property. They should be used as later textual witnesses, not inserted as events already narrated in Genesis 23.
