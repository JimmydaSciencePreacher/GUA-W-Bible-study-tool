## 3. Immediate context — Revelation 22:6–17

The verses immediately preceding the warning provide a sequence that must remain in view:

- **Verses 6–7:** The angel declares that the sayings are faithful and true. The Lord God of the holy prophets sent His angel to show His servants the things which must shortly be done. The coming of the Lord is announced, with a blessing upon the one who keeps the sayings of the prophecy of this book.
- **Verses 8–9:** John identifies himself as the one who heard and saw these things. When he falls down to worship before the angel, the angel refuses worship and commands, “worship God.”
- **Verses 10–11:** John is told not to seal the sayings of the prophecy of this book, for the time is at hand. The passage then states contrasting conditions concerning the unjust and filthy, and the righteous and holy.
- **Verses 12–15:** The coming One declares that He comes quickly and that His reward is with Him, to give every man according as his work shall be. He identifies Himself as Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end, the first and the last. The passage then distinguishes those described as blessed from those described as outside the holy city.
- **Verse 16:** Jesus explicitly identifies Himself: “I Jesus have sent mine angel to testify unto you these things in the churches.”
- **Verse 17:** The Spirit and the bride say, “Come.” The hearer is invited to say, “Come,” and the one who is athirst is invited to come and take the water of life freely.

These are observations of the chapter's stated sequence. They do not, by themselves, settle every interpretive question raised by verses 18–19.

## 4. Observations — Revelation 22:18–21

- Verse 18 begins, “For I testify,” and identifies the audience as every man that hears the words of the prophecy of “this book.”
- Verse 18 states a warning concerning adding to “these things.”
- Verse 19 states a corresponding warning concerning taking away from the words of “the book of this prophecy.”
- The consequences stated in verses 18–19 are expressed as acts of God.
- Verse 20 identifies “He which testifieth these things” and records the declaration, “Surely I come quickly.”
- The words “Amen. Even so, come, Lord Jesus” follow that declaration.
- Verse 21 closes with a statement of the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ and “Amen.”

## 5. Speaker and testimony — what can be established?

The immediate context names more than one participant in the testimony:

- John says that he heard and saw these things (verse 8).
- The angel speaks and gives John instructions (verses 6–11).
- Jesus explicitly identifies Himself in verse 16: “I Jesus have sent mine angel to testify unto you these things in the churches.”
- Verse 18 begins with the first-person statement, “For I testify.”
- Verse 20 speaks of “He which testifieth these things” and records, “Surely I come quickly.”

**Observation:** The first-person wording in verse 18 and the description in verse 20 should not be silently assigned to one speaker without examining the change in wording and the immediate context.

**Inference, not yet a settled textual identification:** John is a possible speaker for the first-person warning in verse 18, given his identification as the one who heard and saw these things in verse 8 and the shift from Jesus' explicit self-identification in verse 16. The text as quoted does not name the speaker of verse 18 in that verse itself. Preserve this as a question for further examination rather than treating the inference as certain.

Verse 20's “He which testifieth these things” is followed by “Surely I come quickly,” and the response addresses the Lord Jesus. The speaker of that promise is therefore identified in the closing exchange with the Lord Jesus.

## 6. What does “this book” refer to?

**Text:** Verses 18 and 19 repeatedly describe the subject as “the prophecy of this book,” “the words of the book of this prophecy,” and “this book.”

**Observation:** The immediate context is the closing section of Revelation. The same chapter refers to “the sayings of the prophecy of this book” in verses 7 and 10, and verse 16 says Jesus sent His angel to testify “these things” in the churches.

**Current inference:** The direct referent in Revelation 22:18–19 is the prophetic book being closed here — Revelation. The passage does not, in these words, explicitly say “the whole Bible.” A broader principle of reverence toward God's words may be considered through other Scriptures, but it must not be substituted for the direct wording and referent of this passage.

**Unresolved:** The relationship between this specific warning and other biblical instructions not to add to or take away from God's words should be examined through their own contexts.
