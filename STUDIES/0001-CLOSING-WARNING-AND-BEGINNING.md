# Study 0001 — The Closing Warning and the Beginning

- **Primary passages:** Revelation 22:18–19; Genesis 1:1
- **Translation:** King James Version (KJV)
- **Study status:** Initial pass; continuing
- **Scope:** Bible study only. No scientific investigation is conducted in this record.

## 1. Why begin here?

The study begins at the closing verses of the Bible, Revelation 22:18–19, before moving to the opening verse, Genesis 1:1. This establishes the study's first discipline: receive the written text carefully; do not add to it, take from it, or make an interpretation equal to the text.

This is the chosen study order. It does not, by itself, establish that Revelation 22:18–19 is a universal statement about every possible act of biblical interpretation. The immediate wording and context must be examined first.

## 2. First passage — Revelation 22:18–19 (KJV)

> **18** For I testify unto every man that heareth the words of the prophecy of this book, If any man shall add unto these things, God shall add unto him the plagues that are written in this book:
>
> **19** And if any man shall take away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God shall take away his part out of the book of life, and out of the holy city, and from the things which are written in this book.

### What the text explicitly says

- The speaker identifies the statement as a testimony.
- The stated audience is every person who hears the words of the prophecy of “this book.”
- A warning is given against adding to “these things.”
- A warning is given against taking away from “the words of the book of this prophecy.”
- The consequences are stated in the passage itself.

### Initial observations

1. The passage contains two corresponding warnings: adding and taking away.
2. The repeated phrase “this book” / “the book of this prophecy” gives the warning an immediate textual object.
3. The passage does not invite the hearer to edit the prophecy according to preference.
4. The warning itself must be read in context; its exact scope should not be enlarged or narrowed by assumption.

### What we must not claim yet

- We must not assume, without further examination, that every interpretive disagreement is an act of adding to or taking away from the text.
- We must not use this warning to judge another person's heart or spiritual state.
- We must not treat our explanation of the warning as if it were the warning itself.

## 3. The four questions — first pass

These questions work together; they are not four sequential stages.

### 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

- What does the passage itself identify as the object being protected?
- What is the immediate context of “this book”?
- How can the study preserve the difference between the written words and a Witness's explanation of those words?

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The governing distinction is Source and derivative witness. The Witness receives and reports; the Witness does not become the Author or acquire authority to alter what was given.

This is a GUA-W framing of the study discipline, not a replacement for the passage's own meaning.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

The text explicitly warns against adding to and taking away from the words of the prophecy of this book. The paired wording makes preservation of the received text a central concern of this passage.

The precise scope and application of the warning remain subject to its context.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

A Witness must distinguish what the text says from what the Witness thinks it means. A report can become inaccurate if the Witness silently inserts, removes, or presents an inference as though it were written in the passage.

A limitation in comprehension is not, by itself, proof of misalignment. The study must correct its own claims without judging another person's heart.

## 4. Transition to the beginning — Genesis 1:1 (KJV)

> **1** In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.

### What the text explicitly says

- The verse begins with the words “In the beginning.”
- God is named as the One who created.
- The stated objects are “the heaven and the earth.”

### Initial observations

1. God is the grammatical subject of “created.”
2. The verse attributes the act of creation to God; it does not name a human or created Witness as its cause.
3. “The heaven and the earth” presents the stated scope of the creation act in this opening declaration.
4. The verse is the opening statement of Genesis; its wording and relationship to the following verses must be examined in sequence.

### GUA-W relationship — carefully bounded

The verse directly names God as Creator. The GUA-W governing order, **Author → Word → Creation → Witness**, may be used as a map for organizing the study, but Genesis 1:1 alone does not explicitly state every term or define every relationship in that sequence. Those connections must be examined through Scripture rather than inserted into this verse.
