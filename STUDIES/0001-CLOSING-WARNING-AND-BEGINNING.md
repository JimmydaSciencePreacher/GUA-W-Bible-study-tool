# Study 0001 — The Closing Warning and the Beginning

- **Primary passages:** Revelation 22:18–19; Genesis 1:1–5
- **Translation:** King James Version (KJV)
- **Study status:** Initial pass through Genesis 1:5; continuing verse by verse
- **Scope:** Bible study only. No scientific investigation is conducted in this record.

## 1. Why begin here?

The study begins at the closing verses of the Bible, Revelation 22:18–19, before moving to the opening account, Genesis 1. This establishes the study's first discipline: receive the written text carefully; do not add to it, take from it, or make an interpretation equal to the text.

## 2. Genesis 1:1–5 — Textual record

**Genesis 1:1 (KJV)**

> In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.

**Genesis 1:2 (KJV)**

> And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.

**Genesis 1:3 (KJV)**

> And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.

**Genesis 1:4 (KJV)**

> And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness.

**Genesis 1:5 (KJV)**

> And God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. And the evening and the morning were the first day.

## 3. Observations: Genesis 1:1–5

### Genesis 1:1

- The text identifies God as the Creator.
- The heaven and the earth are the created subject.
- The verse does not identify another originating cause.

### Genesis 1:2

- The earth is described as “without form, and void.”
- Darkness is upon the face of the deep.
- The Spirit of God moves upon the face of the waters.
- The text records these conditions before the command concerning light in verse 3.

### Genesis 1:3

The verse records a command and its result:

1. God said.
2. The words spoken were, “Let there be light.”
3. “And there was light.”

The result follows the spoken Word. The text does not describe an independent source producing the light.

### Genesis 1:4

The verse records further actions:

1. God saw the light.
2. God saw that it was good.
3. God divided the light from the darkness.

The evaluation belongs to God. The Witness records the declaration; the Witness does not supply the verdict.

### Genesis 1:5

The verse records naming and the designation of the first day:

1. God called the light Day.
2. God called the darkness Night.
3. The evening and the morning were the first day.

The naming is attributed to God. The verse concludes by identifying the evening and morning as the first day. Do not replace this wording with an assumed sequence or an imported definition of day.

## 4. Mapping what is revealed through the creating

A significant clarification emerged during this study.

The map must not be treated as a completed architecture that the Author consults before creating. The Author does not follow the map. The map becomes recognizable to the Witness as the Author's work is revealed.

The repository analogy is a way to understand the study method: imagine recording each operation as it occurs, rather than designing a finished diagram first and forcing the events to fit it. This is an analogy for how the Witness maps the account; Genesis does not say that God literally creates or consults a repository.

**Governing observation:** Creation is the Author's work. GUA-W is the map by which the Witness recognizes and records relationships disclosed through that work.

### Genesis 1:3 as the first mapping point

Genesis 1:3 is the first recorded command-and-result sequence in the account:

**God said → “Let there be light” → “and there was light.”**

Genesis 1:4 then records the Author seeing the light, evaluating it as good, and dividing it from darkness. Genesis 1:5 records the naming of light and darkness and the designation of the first day.

This sequence gives the study its first identifiable point for mapping the relationship between the Author's Word and the resulting created reality.

The phrase **“Birth of GUA-W”** is retained here as the study's interpretive description of the first point at which the map becomes identifiable through the recorded execution. It does not mean that GUA-W is an independent author, cause, or object named in Genesis.

The map follows the Author's work; it does not precede, cause, or govern that work.

A Witness may report faithfully and still have an incomplete account. The proper response to a limitation is further examination, not a claim to the Author's authority or a judgment of another person's heart.
