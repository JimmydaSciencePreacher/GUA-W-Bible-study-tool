## The drying of the earth: observation, distinction, and completion

## 1. Read the passage — KJV

**Genesis 8:13**

> And it came to pass in the six hundredth and first year, in the first month, the first day of the month, the waters were dried up from off the earth: and Noah removed the covering of the ark, and looked, and, behold, the face of the ground was dry.

**Genesis 8:14**

> And in the second month, on the seven and twentieth day of the month, was the earth dried.

## 2. Immediate observations

The passage records two dated statements:

| Verse | Date | What is recorded |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 8:13 | Year 601, first month, first day | The waters are dried from the earth; Noah removes the ark's covering and sees that the ground's surface is dry. |
| Genesis 8:14 | Year 601, second month, twenty-seventh day | The earth is declared dry. |

Three distinct narrative elements should be retained:

1. The waters are described as dried from the earth.
2. Noah removes the covering of the ark, looks, and sees that the surface of the ground is dry.
3. A later date is given at which the earth is declared dry.

The text does not use the dove's earlier observation as a substitute for these later dated statements. Genesis 8:11 records Noah's inference from the olive leaf that the waters had abated; Genesis 8:13–14 records later drying milestones.

**Calendar arithmetic correction and qualification:** If one assumes thirty-day months, as in the conventional calculation of the five-month/150-day interval in Genesis 7:11 and 8:3–4, the interval from the first day of month one to the twenty-seventh day of month two is **56 elapsed days**. Counting both endpoint dates gives 57 calendar dates. This calculation depends on the thirty-day-month assumption; the two dates themselves are explicit. Do not call the interval “57 elapsed days.”

## 3. Hebrew investigation

### 3.1 The verb in Genesis 8:13 — *charav* (חָרַב)

The Hebrew verb in verse 13 is from the root *charav* (חרב), with a semantic range including becoming dry, being dried up, or becoming parched/desolate.

The form *charvu* (חָרְבוּ) appears twice in the verse:

- First, describing the waters being dried from the earth.
- Second, describing the surface of the ground being dry.

The repetition is notable. The narrative first describes the waters, then Noah removes the ark's covering, looks, and sees the ground's surface.

The second occurrence follows *vayar* (“and he saw/looked”) and *ve-hinneh* (“and behold”). The latter draws attention to what Noah sees.

The verb is not a technical hydrology term. Its two occurrences should not be converted into a precise scientific sequence beyond what the narrative supplies.

**Disposition:** The waters and the ground's surface are both described with the *charav* root in verse 13. Noah's direct observation is explicitly narrated.

### 3.2 The verb in Genesis 8:14 — *yavesh* (יָבֵשׁ)

Verse 14 uses a different root, *yabesh* (יבש), meaning to be or become dry. The form *yaveshah* (יָבְשָׁה) is feminine singular, agreeing grammatically with *ha'aretz* (הָאָרֶץ), “the earth” or “the land.”

The distinction between *charav* in verse 13 and *yabesh* in verse 14 is worth recording. However, the roots have overlapping semantic territory involving dryness. The difference alone does not establish two technical stages or two different physical mechanisms.

The stronger distinction is supplied by the narrative itself: verse 13 gives Noah's dated observation of the dry ground's surface; verse 14 gives a later date and declares the earth dry.

**Disposition:** Different Hebrew roots are used in the two verses, but they should not be forced into a technical contrast the text does not define.

### 3.3 The ark's covering — *mikseh* (מִכְסֶה)

The word translated “covering” is *mikseh* (מִכְסֶה), from a root associated with covering. Noah removes it from the ark.

Genesis 8:6 separately says Noah opened the ark's window. The passage does not identify the covering removed in verse 13 as the same feature as that window.

The text does not tell us whether the covering was the entire roof, a removable roof section, or another covering arrangement. Nor does it give enough architectural detail to reconstruct how the covering was removed.

**Disposition:** Noah removes an ark covering and looks outside. Its precise construction and relation to the window in 8:6 remain unresolved.

### 3.4 *Ha'aretz* and *ha'adamah* — earth/land and ground

Verse 13 uses both *ha'aretz* (הָאָרֶץ) and *ha'adamah* (הָאֲדָמָה):

- *Ha'aretz* is translated “earth” in the statement about the waters.
- *Ha'adamah* is translated “ground” in the statement about the surface Noah sees.

Verse 14 again uses *ha'aretz* for the earth/land.

The terms overlap in their possible reference to land or ground, but their use in the verse gives the narrative a movement from the waters over the earth to the visible surface of the ground, and then to the later declaration about the earth.

Do not impose a rigid modern geological distinction between “earth” and “soil” on these Hebrew nouns.

**Disposition:** The wording moves between the land/earth and the ground's visible surface. The text does not provide a modern technical soil classification.

## 4. Chronology in the Flood narrative

The dates in Genesis 7–8 establish a sequence:

| Passage | Date | Recorded event |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 7:11 | Year 600, month 2, day 17 | The Flood begins. |
| Genesis 8:4 | Year 600, month 7, day 17 | The ark rests on the mountains of Ararat. |
| Genesis 8:5 | Year 600, month 10, day 1 | Mountain tops become visible. |
| Genesis 8:13 | Year 601, month 1, day 1 | Waters are dried from the earth; Noah observes the ground's surface is dry. |
| Genesis 8:14 | Year 601, month 2, day 27 | The earth is declared dry. |

Genesis 8:13 explicitly dates the first day of the first month in Noah's six hundred and first year. This is a new-year marker in the narrative chronology.

The second date is the twenty-seventh day of the second month. The passage gives no additional explanation of what changed during the interval.

The thirty-day-month convention is supported as a working calculation by the relationship between the second month, seventeenth day (Genesis 7:11) and seventh month, seventeenth day (Genesis 8:4), together with the 150 days in Genesis 7:24 and 8:3. It remains a calendar model for interpreting the chronology, not an independently supplied statement that every month in every relevant calendar system had exactly thirty days.

**Disposition:** The dates are textual data. The 56-day interval is a calculation conditional on thirty-day months. The exact environmental changes during that interval are not described.

## 5. Creation and scientific investigation

### 5.1 Surface water and soil moisture are not the same thing

The physical distinction between surface water and water retained in soil is directly relevant to this passage.

When land is inundated, water can cover the surface and fill soil pore spaces. After surface water recedes, gravity can drain some water downward through the soil profile. Water remains in smaller pores and can subsequently be removed through evaporation and plant uptake.

Penn State Extension explains that saturated soil loses some water through gravitational drainage; after drainage, the soil may reach what soil scientists call *field capacity*. Water retained at or below field capacity can be removed through plant uptake and evaporation. The amount and rate vary with soil properties.

The U.S. Bureau of Reclamation's Flood Hydrology Manual explains infiltration and describes how soil wetness, compaction, cracks, organic matter, swelling clay, root holes, and other factors affect the movement of water into soil.

These sources establish a general physical distinction:
- Surface water can recede or disappear.
- Soil may still retain substantial moisture.
- The visible surface and deeper soil can be in different moisture conditions.

This is relevant context, not a reconstruction of Noah's environment.

**Disposition:** Surface drying and deeper soil drying are physically distinguishable. The text does not quantify either condition.

### 5.2 What affects the drying of land?

Relevant physical variables include:

- **Soil texture:** Sand, silt, and clay differ in pore structure, permeability, and water retention.
- **Drainage and topography:** Slope, depressions, and subsurface pathways affect where water collects and moves.
- **Temperature and solar energy:** These affect evaporation.
- **Wind and atmospheric humidity:** These affect the rate of evaporation from exposed surfaces.
- **Vegetation:** Once present and active, plants can remove water through transpiration.
- **Soil structure and compaction:** These affect infiltration and drainage.

The Genesis passage supplies no measurements for these variables. It does not describe the soil types, temperature, wind speed, humidity, drainage pathways, or plant cover at this stage.

Therefore, no reliable drying-rate calculation can be made from the passage alone.

**Disposition:** Modern hydrology identifies relevant mechanisms and variables, but does not establish which mechanisms dominated the drying in Genesis 8.

### 5.3 Why the two dates are scientifically coherent

The narrative's dated observations are compatible with the general physical distinction between the recession of surface water, the appearance of a dry surface, and broader drying of land.

The first date records the waters dried from the earth and Noah's observation that the ground's surface was dry. A later date records the earth as dry.

The text does not define “dry” in terms of soil moisture percentage, bearing capacity, sediment stability, or agricultural readiness. It does not say that every subsurface layer had reached equilibrium moisture or that the land was ready for cultivation.

The later command to leave the ark is recorded in Genesis 8:15–19, not in verses 13–14.

**Disposition:** The sequence is physically coherent without specifying a complete mechanism. Do not add technical meanings to “dry” that the text does not provide.

### 5.4 What cannot be reconstructed

The passage does not tell us:
- the depth or distribution of water remaining on the first date;
- whether all low-lying areas were dry;
- the soil texture or depth of saturation;
- the rate of groundwater drainage;
- whether evaporation, drainage, or other processes dominated;
- whether vegetation had recovered across the land;
- the ground's load-bearing capacity;
- the precise condition that made the earth ready for Noah's departure.

No modern flood, soil experiment, or hydrological model can be identified as a reconstruction of this event merely because it demonstrates a possible process.

**Disposition:** Science supplies relevant mechanisms and limits; the historical environmental details remain unresolved.

## 11. Findings and open questions

### Established by the text
- The first date is the first day of the first month in Noah's six hundred and first year.
- The waters are described as dried from the earth.
- Noah removes the covering of the ark.
- Noah looks and sees that the surface of the ground is dry.
- The second date is the twenty-seventh day of the second month.
- On that later date, the earth is declared dry.

### Supported interpretations
- The two dated statements preserve a progression in the narrative.
- Surface water recession and deeper soil drying are physically distinguishable.
- Noah's direct observation is distinct from the dove's earlier indirect evidence.
- The later declaration that the earth is dry is distinct from the later command to leave the ark.

### Unresolved questions
1. What precise structure is meant by the ark's *mikseh* covering?
2. How does the covering relate architecturally to the window opened in Genesis 8:6?
3. What physical conditions existed on the first date when Noah observed the ground's surface?
4. What changes occurred during the interval before the second date?
5. What roles, if any, did drainage, infiltration, evaporation, and wind play?
6. Does “the earth was dry” describe a condition suitable for travel, habitation, or some other threshold? The text does not define a technical threshold.
7. What was the condition of deeper soil and groundwater?
8. How does the narrative's chronology relate to differing ancient textual traditions and calendar assumptions?

### What must not be claimed
- That Genesis specifies a 56-day drying mechanism.
- That every month in every calendar system was necessarily thirty days.
- That the whole soil profile was dry on the first date.
- That the earth was agriculturally ready on the second date.
- That a modern hydrological model reconstructs the Flood.
- That Noah's observation was itself permission to leave the ark.
- That the two Hebrew roots alone prove two technical stages of drying.
